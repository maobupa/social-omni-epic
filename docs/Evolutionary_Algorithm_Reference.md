Here's the intuition, mechanism by mechanism, framed around what each one is *afraid of* — because every algorithm in this lineage was invented to escape a specific failure, and your system inherits both the escapes and the fears.

**The founding fear: objectives are deceptive.** Start here because everything descends from it. If you optimize directly for a goal, you climb the nearest hill — and the nearest hill is usually not the mountain. **Picbreeder** (humans breeding images) found that the path to a recognizable car went *through* images that looked nothing like cars; nobody aiming for "car" would ever have selected those ancestors. **Novelty Search** then made the radical move: drop the objective entirely, reward only "is this different from what I've seen," and on deceptive problems it *beats* goal-directed search. The lesson you inherit: a stepping stone doesn't resemble the destination, so a system that only rewards things resembling success will saw off the branch it needs to climb. This is *why* your Thompson reward is learning-progress rather than solved-ness — "solved" is the deceptive objective; "produced learning" is the honest one that keeps stepping stones alive.

**Quality-Diversity (MAP-Elites): stop hill-climbing, start illuminating.** The fear here is that even good search collapses onto one peak and goes blind to the rest of the space. MAP-Elites refuses to keep a single "best." It divides the space into niches by *behavioral* descriptors — not "how good" but "what kind" — and keeps the best individual *in each niche*, selecting parents uniformly across niches. The result isn't one champion; it's a lit-up map of "the best version of every kind of thing." Your niching is exactly this: niches over social *dynamics* (not settings), so coverage means "we found the hardest learnable scenario of each structural type," not "we found ninety variations of the cofounder fight." The two-level Thompson you deferred is literally MAP-Elites parent selection bolted onto your bandit.

**Minimal Criterion Coevolution: viability, not maximization, as the gate.** The fear: if you admit environments by "how hard," you drift to impossible; by "how easy," to trivial. MCC's answer is a *criterion*, not an optimum — a maze is admitted only if some agent can solve it, an agent only if it solves some maze. Reciprocal viability. Your minimal criterion is the two-sided band: a scenario counts only if the learner *failed attempt 1* (hard enough to matter) *and LP > 0* (learnable enough to teach). You're not maximizing difficulty; you're admitting the viable middle.

**POET: coevolve the problem and the solver, and transfer between them.** This is your closest structural ancestor. POET's fear is that a fixed curriculum either outpaces the agent (impossible) or lags it (boring) — so it evolves environments and agents *together*, admits new environments only inside a difficulty band relative to current agents (that band is your Outcome-3 frontier, exactly), and — the crucial part — lets a skill learned in one environment *transfer* to unlock another. Your chronicle inheritance along lineages **is** POET transfer, done in language instead of network weights: the skills a learner abstracts on a parent scenario ride forward to its harder children. The coevolution is real even though your learner's "weights" never change — capability moves through the chronicle, not the parameters.

**Darwin-Gödel Machine: keep the whole archive, judge parents by their descendants.** The fear: greedily keeping only your current best throws away the mediocre individual who happens to be the only viable ancestor of something great. DGM keeps every viable variant and selects parents by performance *and* by how many novel, productive offspring they've spawned. This is precisely your reframe of the Thompson reward — an anchor's fitness is its *children's* learning progress, not its own outcome. A bland scenario that reliably spawns frontier children is a stepping stone, and you credit it as one.

**AlphaEvolve: the LLM is the mutation operator, and the evaluator is the real battleground.** Classical EAs mutate by random perturbation. AlphaEvolve (and you) mutate by *asking a language model to propose a variant* — vastly smarter moves than random, because the model carries priors about what's plausible. But it surfaces the lurking danger of the whole paradigm: **evolution exploits any slack in the evaluator.** If your judge can be fooled, the search *will* find the scenarios that fool it rather than the ones that teach. That's why so much of your spec is evaluator-hardening — cross-lab judge, key-aware checks, pairwise-with-swap LP, fail-closed gates. In an evolutionary system the evaluator isn't a detail; it's the fitness function, and a corruptible fitness function corrupts everything downstream.

**OMNI-EPIC: let a foundation model be the arbiter of "interesting and learnable."** The older algorithms hand-crafted their novelty measures and difficulty criteria — and Enhanced POET warned that hand-crafted descriptors *cap* open-endedness, because you can only search the dimensions you thought to define. OMNI's bet: a foundation model has absorbed human notions of interestingness, so let *it* judge, and you escape the hand-crafting ceiling. Your inheritance is direct, plus a domain argument that strengthens it: in the *social* domain, interestingness is irreducibly cultural and contextual — there's no formula for "socially meaningful" the way there's a formula for maze-novelty — so the FM-as-arbiter move is *more* justified for you than it was for physical environments, not less.

Now stand back and see the assembly, because the synthesis is the contribution: **the scenario is the evolving organism; FM generation is mutation (POET/AlphaEvolve); the theory-grounded triple-plus-key is the genome that makes mutations structured rather than random; learning progress is fitness, and it's a non-deceptive fitness that protects stepping stones (Novelty Search/DGM); the minimal criterion is the viability gate (MCC); niches keep the search illuminating the whole space rather than climbing one peak (MAP-Elites); the chronicle is the transfer channel that makes it genuine coevolution (POET); and the FM judges interestingness because the domain has no formula for it (OMNI), which puts the evaluator's integrity at the center of the design (AlphaEvolve).**

The one honest asymmetry to keep in view: in classical coevolution the *solver* changes — its weights mutate and are selected. In yours, the learner's weights are frozen; what evolves and what accumulates capability is the *scenario population and its attached skill chronicles*. That's not a flaw, but it's the precise spot where your system is novel rather than derivative, and naming it cleanly ("coevolution of scenarios and skills under a fixed base policy, with capability carried by retrieved knowledge rather than parameter updates") is what separates "we instantiated POET in text" from "we built something the lineage hadn't." Lean on the lineage for legitimacy; lean on that asymmetry for originality.
